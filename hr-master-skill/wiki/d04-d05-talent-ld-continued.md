# HR Wiki — Domain 4-5 Continuation (Concepts 71-104)

## 71. Strategic Onboarding
**List:** A
**Definition:** A planned, multi-month process integrating new hires into role, team, and culture — encompassing pre-boarding, orientation, role clarity, relationship building, and 30/60/90-day milestones.
**Application:** Best practice: pre-boarding starts at offer acceptance (welcome materials, equipment, system access); Day 1 focused on welcome and connection; Week 1 on role clarity and stakeholder introductions; Month 1 on goal setting; 30/60/90 reviews with manager; Buddy programme for peer integration; first-90-day survey.
**Expert insight:** Bersin's research: strong onboarding improves new hire retention by 82% and productivity by 70%. Aberdeen Group: 86% of new hires decide within 6 months whether to stay long-term. Gallup: only 12% of employees strongly agree their company does onboarding well.
**Takeaways:**
- Onboarding is a 90-day-plus journey, not a Day 1 event.
- Manager involvement is the single biggest predictor of success.
- Track new hire NPS and retention to 12 months.

## 72. Probationary Review Processes
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** A structured assessment at the end of an initial employment period (typically 3-6 months in UK; "at-will" in US) determining whether to confirm employment or terminate.
**Application:** Components: clear probation criteria stated at offer; regular check-ins during period; mid-probation formal review; end-of-probation decision with documented rationale; outcomes — confirm, extend, terminate. UK specifics: even within probation, employees have protections against discrimination and require ACAS-aligned processes.
**Case Study:** A UK SaaS firm hired a senior product manager on a 6-month probation. At month 4, performance gaps were evident, but the manager avoided difficult conversations. At month 6, the employee was abruptly dismissed without prior formal feedback. A tribunal awarded £24K for unfair procedure under the ACAS Code, despite probation status. Remediation: introduced mandatory mid-probation reviews with written feedback, manager training on probation conversations, and a 30/60/90-day check-in protocol. Cost of getting it wrong: 12x the cost of doing probation properly.
**Expert insight:** ACAS Guidance: probation does not waive procedural fairness. CIPD: 26% of organisations report poorly managed probations contribute to early attrition and tribunal risk.
**Takeaways:**
- Document feedback continuously during probation.
- A surprise termination at month 6 is rarely defensible.
- Train managers on the conversation, not just the process.

## 73. Build vs Buy Talent Strategy
**List:** A
**Definition:** Strategic decision between developing internal talent ("build") and hiring external talent ("buy") — or combining both ("borrow" via contractors/contingent staff; "bot" via automation).
**Application:** Decision framework: speed (buy if urgent), specialty (buy if rare), strategic (build if core), cost (build cheaper long-term), culture (build for cultural fit). Workforce planning identifies the right mix per role family. Tech firms typically buy senior engineering; consultancies typically build via apprenticeship; financial services hybrid.
**Expert insight:** Peter Cappelli (Wharton) argues most firms over-buy and under-build, ignoring sunk training costs and cultural dilution. Mercer's research: ratio of internal vs external moves correlates with engagement and retention.
**Takeaways:**
- Strategic roles → build; specialty roles → buy.
- Internal mobility (job markets) bridges build/buy.
- "Build" requires upfront L&D investment most firms underestimate.

## 74. Employment Contracts — Types & Terms
**List:** A
**Definition:** Legal agreements defining the employer-employee relationship — covering type (permanent/fixed-term/zero-hours/agency/casual), express terms (written), implied terms (custom/statute), and restrictive covenants (non-compete/non-solicit).
**Application:** Contract types fit different needs: permanent for stability; fixed-term for projects; zero-hours for variable demand (UK regulated post-2015); agency for flexibility (UK Agency Worker Regulations 2010 — equal treatment after 12 weeks). Restrictive covenants must be "reasonable in scope" to be enforceable.
**Expert insight:** ACAS: most contract disputes stem from unclear implied terms (custom/practice) — codify explicitly. UK Supreme Court Uber case (2021) reshaped worker status — many "self-employed" workers are now "workers" with rights to NMW and holiday pay.
**Takeaways:**
- Choose contract type strategically, not for cost optimisation alone.
- Restrictive covenants narrowly drafted are more likely enforceable.
- Worker status case law is evolving rapidly — review regularly.

## 75. IR35 & Worker Status / Gig Economy
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** UK tax legislation determining whether contractors are genuinely self-employed or "deemed employees" for tax purposes. Worker status classification affects rights (NMW, holiday, sick pay) and tax (PAYE).
**Application:** IR35 since April 2021: medium/large private-sector clients are responsible for determining contractor status. Tests: Mutuality of Obligation, Personal Service, Control, Integration, Financial Risk. Status Determination Statements (SDS) must be issued; CEST tool from HMRC.
**Case Study:** A UK FTSE-250 retailer reviewed 480 contractors under IR35. Initial determination: 70% inside IR35. The CHRO faced cost increase of £2.3M (employer NIC). Strategic response: (1) terminated 90 contractors no longer commercial; (2) converted 180 to fixed-term employees (retaining critical skills); (3) renegotiated terms with 50 genuinely outside IR35. Six months later: HMRC compliance verified, cost increase contained to £1.4M, retention of critical capability achieved.
**Expert insight:** CIPD's gig economy research: 70% of "self-employed" workers in low-skilled gig work would prefer employee status. The Supreme Court Uber ruling (2021) is the leading authority. AHK Recruitment: 33% of contractors left the market post-IR35 reform.
**Takeaways:**
- Status determinations have major financial implications.
- "Inside IR35" doesn't mean "must convert to PAYE" — multiple options exist.
- Misclassification penalties are severe — get it right.

## 76. Visa & Immigration Management
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Compliance with national immigration law for hiring foreign workers — covering visa categories (H-1B/L-1/O-1/TN in US; Skilled Worker/Senior Specialist in UK), sponsorship licences, RTW checks, and ongoing compliance.
**Application:** US framework: I-9 verification required for all hires; E-Verify mandatory in some states; H-1B subject to annual cap (85,000) and lottery. UK framework: Sponsor Licence required (cost £1,476-£3,000); Skilled Worker visa requires £38,700 salary (2024); Certificate of Sponsorship per role. Record-keeping mandatory.
**Case Study:** A US tech firm had 200 H-1B engineers. In FY 2023, lottery selection rate fell to 14%. Strategic response: (1) opened a Canadian engineering hub (Express Entry pathway); (2) shifted to O-1 visas for senior researchers (no cap); (3) increased L-1B intra-company transfers from India and UK offices. Result: critical hires preserved without H-1B dependency. CHRO presented immigration strategy as a board-level supply chain risk.
**Expert insight:** Fragomen (immigration law leaders): post-2020 immigration restrictions have made geographic diversification of talent hubs a strategic imperative. CIPD UK Sponsorship guidance: 40% of new sponsor licence applications fail due to procedural gaps.
**Takeaways:**
- Immigration is workforce supply chain — treat strategically.
- Diversify visa pathways and geographic hubs.
- Compliance failures can trigger sponsor licence revocation.

## 77. Training Needs Analysis (TNA)
**List:** A
**Definition:** A structured assessment of the gap between current and required capabilities at three levels: organisational (strategic skills needs), operational/task (job requirements), and individual (person-level gaps).
**Application:** Methods: organisational level — strategy analysis, KPI underperformance, environmental scanning; task level — competency frameworks, job analysis, observation; individual level — performance reviews, 360s, self-assessment, psychometrics. Output: prioritised TNA matrix linking learner needs to organisational goals.
**Expert insight:** Boydell & Leary's (1996) "Three-Level TNA" remains the standard. Will Thalheimer's research: TNA done at all three levels yields 4-5x higher transfer-of-training. CIPD's L&D Survey: only 33% of organisations do systematic TNA at task level.
**Takeaways:**
- TNA at one level only is incomplete.
- Skills gap data should drive learning prioritisation, not learner preference.
- Refresh TNA annually — skills decay and emerge.

## 78. ADDIE Model
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Instructional design framework: Analysis (TNA, audience, context), Design (objectives, structure, assessments), Development (build content), Implementation (deliver), Evaluation (Kirkpatrick levels).
**Application:** Standard L&D project sequence: ADDIE phases run linearly or iteratively. Each phase has deliverables: Analysis → needs report; Design → design document/storyboard; Development → completed content; Implementation → trained learners; Evaluation → impact measurement.
**Case Study:** A pharmaceutical company piloted a sales rep training overhaul. The first attempt skipped formal Analysis (assumed needs from anecdote) and Evaluation (no measurement). Reps disengaged; sales did not improve. The redo applied full ADDIE: Analysis identified specific objection-handling and product knowledge gaps; Design used scenario-based microlearning; Development included VR-based practice; Implementation paced over 8 weeks; Evaluation showed 18% objection-resolution improvement and £3.2M attributable sales lift over 12 months.
**Expert insight:** ADDIE has been criticised as too linear for agile environments — hence emergence of SAM (Successive Approximation Model). Cathy Moore's Action Mapping approach emphasises starting from business outcome backwards.
**Takeaways:**
- ADDIE failure usually traces to skipped Analysis or Evaluation.
- Iterative ADDIE (Agile-style) is more practical than waterfall.
- Don't equate "training delivered" with "value created."

## 79. SAM — Successive Approximation Model
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Michael Allen's iterative instructional design model — alternative to ADDIE — emphasising rapid prototyping, learner feedback, and successive refinement. Phases: Preparation → Iterative Design → Iterative Development.
**Application:** SAM uses small "Savvy Start" workshops to prototype quickly, get learner feedback within days (not months), and iterate. Better suited to digital learning, agile environments, and learner-centric design. Compared to ADDIE: faster, more learner-input, less polished initial outputs.
**Case Study:** A retail chain needed onboarding redesign for 12,000 store staff. ADDIE estimated 9 months; SAM delivered in 14 weeks. Process: 1-day Savvy Start with stakeholders + 5 frontline staff; 2-week prototype; pilot with 30 stores; iterate; full rollout. Engagement scores rose 40% vs prior onboarding. Key learning: SAM's early-learner feedback caught 3 major content errors that would have shipped in ADDIE.
**Expert insight:** Allen's *Leaving ADDIE for SAM* (2012) is the foundational text. ATD research: 65% of L&D teams now use SAM-style or Agile approaches alongside ADDIE.
**Takeaways:**
- SAM is faster and more learner-centric than ADDIE.
- Best for digital, learner-facing content.
- ADDIE still valuable for high-stakes, regulated training.

## 80. Bloom's Taxonomy
**List:** A
**Definition:** A hierarchy of cognitive learning objectives (Bloom, 1956; revised Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001): Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate → Create. Higher levels build on lower.
**Application:** Use to design learning objectives at the right cognitive level. Compliance training = Remember/Understand; skills training = Apply/Analyse; leadership development = Evaluate/Create. Assessments must align: don't test "apply" via multiple choice. Map all training to the taxonomy to ensure cognitive depth.
**Expert insight:** Revised taxonomy added "knowledge dimensions" (factual, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive). Marzano's New Taxonomy extends to metacognitive and self-system levels. Widely embedded in instructional design certifications.
**Takeaways:**
- Match assessment to cognitive level — don't test "create" with quizzes.
- Most corporate L&D underweights Evaluate and Create.
- Bloom's verbs (e.g., "demonstrate", "analyse") guide objective writing.

## 81. Andragogy / Knowles Adult Learning
**List:** A
**Definition:** Malcolm Knowles's six principles of adult learning: adults need to know why; bring experience as resource; are self-directed; learn for problem-solving; are motivated by relevance; respond to intrinsic over extrinsic motivation.
**Application:** Design implications: explain why this matters (don't assume); leverage learner experience as content (cases, peer learning); enable self-direction (LMS choices, learning paths); use problem-based learning; tie to learner's real challenges; emphasise meaning over rewards.
**Expert insight:** Knowles's *The Adult Learner* (8th ed., 2020) is foundational. Critique: distinction between pedagogy and andragogy is over-stated — many "adult" principles apply to learners of any age. But the principles remain valuable design heuristics.
**Takeaways:**
- Adults learn best when they see why and use experience.
- Self-directed learning paths boost engagement.
- Problem-based design outperforms content-delivery design.

## 82. Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle
**List:** A
**Definition:** David Kolb's four-stage cycle: Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualisation → Active Experimentation → (loops back). Plus four learning styles (Diverging/Assimilating/Converging/Accommodating).
**Application:** Design L&D to traverse the full cycle: simulations (CE); reflection prompts (RO); models/frameworks (AC); application planning (AE). Many corporate training stops at AC (model teaching) and skips RO and AE — explaining poor transfer to work.
**Expert insight:** Kolb's *Experiential Learning* (1984) integrates Dewey, Lewin, and Piaget. Learning Styles Inventory (LSI) has been criticised for low test-retest reliability — use the cycle as design heuristic, not the inventory for sorting people.
**Takeaways:**
- Design learning to complete the full cycle.
- Reflection and experimentation are the most-neglected stages.
- Skip "learning styles" sorting — focus on cycle completion.

## 83. Learning Theories — Behaviourism, Cognitivism, Constructivism
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Three philosophies underpinning instructional design. Behaviourism (Skinner): learning = behaviour change via stimulus-response. Cognitivism (Bruner, Piaget): learning = mental model building. Constructivism (Vygotsky): learning = social, contextual meaning-making.
**Application:** Different fits: Behaviourism — compliance, safety training, drill-based skills; Cognitivism — knowledge-heavy content, problem-solving; Constructivism — collaborative learning, leadership development, real-world projects. Most modern L&D blends all three.
**Case Study:** A bank designed customer service training using a single-paradigm approach (heavy behaviourist — rules, scripts, drills). Mystery shopping showed compliance but no improvement in customer satisfaction. Redesign blended paradigms: scripts retained (behaviourist core) + situational case discussions (cognitivist) + peer coaching circles where reps shared real customer interactions (constructivist). Customer satisfaction scores rose 14 points in 9 months.
**Expert insight:** Reigeluth's *Instructional-Design Theories and Models* synthesises across paradigms. Will Thalheimer: paradigms are lenses, not religions — blend by audience and outcome.
**Takeaways:**
- No single paradigm fits all needs.
- Compliance ≠ capability — script-only training fails complex tasks.
- Constructivist methods drive durable behaviour change.

## 84. 70-20-10 Learning Model
**List:** A
**Definition:** A heuristic from Center for Creative Leadership research suggesting ~70% of development comes from on-the-job experience, ~20% from social/coaching, ~10% from formal training.
**Application:** Design balanced development plans: stretch assignments and projects (70); mentoring, coaching, peer learning (20); courses, conferences, certifications (10). Use to argue against over-relying on classroom-based L&D. Customise ratios by role and learner.
**Expert insight:** Originated at CCL via Lombardo & Eichinger (1996). Charles Jennings (70-20-10 Institute) operationalised it for L&D practice. Critique: ratios are not validated empirically — treat as design heuristic, not law. CIPD: useful as a planning lens, not measurement.
**Takeaways:**
- Use as design balance, not as a measurement target.
- Most learning happens informally — invest in informal channels.
- Stretch assignments need scaffolding to be developmental.

## 85. Microlearning, Blended, E-learning, M-learning
**List:** A
**Definition:** Microlearning = bite-sized (3-15 min) learning units. Blended = mix of digital and in-person. E-learning = digital self-paced. M-learning = mobile-first delivery.
**Application:** Microlearning fits just-in-time skills and reinforcement. Blended balances digital efficiency with in-person depth. E-learning scales globally. M-learning reaches deskless workers (60% of global workforce). Choose modality by content type, learner context, and learning objective.
**Expert insight:** Bersin's research: microlearning has 17% better engagement than traditional. Deloitte: blended learning yields 65% better skill retention than purely classroom. McKinsey: deskless workforce learning is underinvested by 5x relative to office workers.
**Takeaways:**
- Modality matters less than design quality.
- Mobile-first is essential for non-office workforces.
- Microlearning excels at reinforcement, not new complex skills.

## 86. LMS & LXP
**List:** A
**Definition:** LMS (Learning Management System) — structured platform for delivery and tracking of mandatory/structured content. LXP (Learning Experience Platform) — Netflix-style discovery platform emphasising learner choice, social features, and AI-curated content.
**Application:** Both required: LMS for compliance and structured pathways (e.g., onboarding); LXP for self-directed development. Major LMS: Cornerstone, SuccessFactors, Workday Learning. Major LXP: Degreed, EdCast, 360Learning, Udemy Business.
**Expert insight:** Bersin: LMS-only firms have 50% lower engagement with learning than LXP-enabled firms. Josh Bersin's "Learning in the Flow of Work" research argues both must integrate with work tools (Slack, Teams, Outlook).
**Takeaways:**
- LMS + LXP complement, not replace each other.
- Integration with work tools is the next frontier.
- AI-curated learning is now table-stakes in LXPs.

## 87. AI-Driven Adaptive Learning
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Learning experiences that dynamically personalise content, pacing, and difficulty based on learner data — using AI/ML models trained on engagement, performance, and outcome data.
**Application:** Capabilities: adaptive sequencing (skip mastered topics); difficulty calibration; personalised content recommendations; AI tutors (chatbots like ChatGPT-powered coaches); skill gap diagnostics. Major vendors: Docebo, Cornerstone, Degreed (Beam), Pearson, Squirrel AI.
**Case Study:** A global engineering firm with 45,000 staff piloted AI-adaptive technical training. Baseline: average 32 hours to complete a CAD certification. AI-adaptive version: average 19 hours (40% reduction) with equal or better mastery scores. Engagement increased 28%. Cost savings: £1.8M annual productivity. Risk identified: AI recommendation patterns showed gender bias (steering women to "softer" topics) — required de-biasing intervention.
**Expert insight:** Donald Clark (Wildfire Learning): adaptive learning is the biggest L&D shift since e-learning. Brandon Hall Group: 64% of L&D leaders plan to deploy AI tutors by 2026.
**Takeaways:**
- Adaptive learning compresses time and personalises depth.
- Bias auditing of recommendation engines is mandatory.
- Treat AI as augmentation of human L&D, not replacement.

## 88. Gamification, Simulations, AR/VR in Learning
**List:** A
**Definition:** Gamification = game elements (points, badges, leaderboards) applied to non-game contexts. Simulations = practice environments mimicking real situations. AR/VR = immersive environments via headsets or overlays.
**Application:** Fit by purpose: gamification — motivation, engagement, repeated practice; simulations — risk-free skill practice (sales calls, leadership scenarios, surgical training); AR/VR — high-fidelity simulations (Walmart warehouse training, medical procedures, dangerous environments).
**Expert insight:** PwC's VR study: VR-trained employees were 4x faster to train, 275% more confident in applying skills, and 3.75x more emotionally connected to content than e-learners. Karl Kapp's *Gamification of Learning* is the foundational practitioner text.
**Takeaways:**
- VR delivers transformative results for procedural/spatial skills.
- Gamification fails when extrinsic rewards displace intrinsic motivation.
- Cost is declining rapidly — feasibility expanding.

## 89. On-the-Job Training, Job Rotation, Shadowing
**List:** A
**Definition:** Experiential learning methods: OJT (learning by doing under supervision); Job Rotation (planned moves between roles for breadth); Shadowing (observing experienced practitioners).
**Application:** OJT is ubiquitous but often informal — structure with checklists, coaching, and milestones. Job Rotation typically 3-12 months per assignment; used for high-potentials and graduate schemes. Shadowing valuable for senior role onboarding and cross-functional empathy.
**Expert insight:** CCL's 70-20-10 research roots experiential learning in challenge, novelty, and stretch. Companies like P&G, Unilever, and GE built market-leading talent pipelines via systematic rotation. McKinsey research: 5+ years of varied experience predicts executive readiness better than tenure or training.
**Takeaways:**
- OJT works best when structured, not implicit.
- Rotation builds enterprise perspective and reduces silo-thinking.
- Shadowing is cheap and underused.

## 90. Coaching & Mentoring Programs
**List:** A
**Definition:** Coaching = goal-focused, time-bound development conversations led by trained coaches. Mentoring = longer-term, relationship-based guidance from senior colleagues.
**Application:** Coaching: external for executives; internal coaches for managers; coaching cultures (manager-as-coach). Mentoring: formal programmes (matching, training, structure) and informal sponsorship. ICF/EMCC accreditation standards for coaches; CIPD coaching certifications.
**Expert insight:** ICF Global Coaching Study (2023): coaching delivers 7x ROI on average. Marshall Goldsmith's stakeholder coaching method has demonstrated executive behaviour change. Sponsorship (active advocacy) outperforms mentoring (advisory) for advancement (Sylvia Ann Hewlett research).
**Takeaways:**
- Coaching and mentoring address different needs.
- Sponsorship (acting on behalf of) > mentoring for career advancement.
- Manager-as-coach capability is L&D's biggest ROI lever.

## 91. Action Learning Sets
**List:** A
**Definition:** Reg Revans's method: small groups (5-7) tackle real work problems, using structured peer questioning to develop both task solutions and personal learning. Cycle: present problem → group questioning → action → reflection.
**Application:** Best for: leadership development, cross-functional learning, complex ambiguous problems. Typical structure: 6-12 month programme, monthly sets of 3-4 hours, with a facilitator. Each participant brings own problem; group helps via questions, not answers.
**Expert insight:** Revans's "L = P + Q" (Learning = Programmed knowledge + Questioning insight) is foundational. CIPD: action learning consistently rated highest-impact development method in L&D Surveys. Particularly valuable for senior leaders solving novel problems.
**Takeaways:**
- Real problems > case studies for senior development.
- Skilled facilitation is the make-or-break.
- Confidentiality and discipline distinguish action learning from "vent groups."

## 92. Communities of Practice — Wenger
**List:** A
**Definition:** Etienne Wenger's concept: groups of people sharing a passion, expertise, and practice, who deepen knowledge through ongoing interaction. Defined by Domain (shared interest), Community (relationships), Practice (shared work).
**Application:** Enable CoPs: protect time and space; facilitate (don't manage); provide platforms (Slack channels, Teams groups, internal forums); recognise contributions; connect to business priorities. Many CoPs are self-organising — HR's role is enabling, not controlling.
**Expert insight:** Wenger's *Communities of Practice* (1998) is foundational. World Bank's CoP-based knowledge management (1990s-2000s) is a landmark case. Modern variant: "Guilds" in Spotify's model. CoPs especially valuable for distributed/hybrid organisations.
**Takeaways:**
- Communities are cultivated, not commanded.
- The best CoPs solve real problems, not just network.
- HR's role is the gardener, not the conductor.

## 93. Kirkpatrick's 4 Levels of Evaluation
**List:** A
**Definition:** Donald Kirkpatrick's evaluation framework: Level 1 — Reaction (did learners like it?); Level 2 — Learning (did they learn?); Level 3 — Behaviour (did behaviour change on the job?); Level 4 — Results (did business outcomes improve?).
**Application:** Most L&D measures only Levels 1-2 (smile sheets, quizzes); Levels 3-4 require sustained measurement (behaviour observation, business KPI tracking). Design evaluation at programme design phase, not after delivery.
**Expert insight:** Kirkpatrick (1959, revised by son James) remains the dominant L&D evaluation framework. ATD research: only 22% of organisations measure Level 3; 8% measure Level 4. The "Kirkpatrick Gap" is L&D's biggest credibility problem.
**Takeaways:**
- Most L&D under-measures — Levels 3-4 build credibility.
- Build evaluation into design, not afterthought.
- Behaviour change at scale requires structural reinforcement.

## 94. Phillips ROI Model — 5th Level
**List:** A
**Definition:** Jack Phillips's extension of Kirkpatrick: Level 5 — ROI, calculated as (Programme Benefits − Programme Costs) ÷ Costs × 100, isolating L&D's contribution from other factors.
**Application:** ROI methodology: isolate effects (control groups, trend analysis, estimation); convert to money; calculate ROI. Best applied to specific high-impact programmes, not all training. Phillips's research: leadership development averages 200-700% ROI when properly measured.
**Expert insight:** Phillips ROI Institute has published 5,000+ case studies. Industry-leading rigour for L&D economics. Critique: methodology is intensive — best for flagship programmes, not all training.
**Takeaways:**
- ROI methodology builds L&D credibility with finance.
- Apply selectively — not all training warrants Level 5.
- Isolation methodology is the key (and hardest) step.

## 95. Anderson Value of Learning Model
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Valerie Anderson's (CIPD) three-stage model: Stage 1 — align development to organisational priorities; Stage 2 — evaluate contribution; Stage 3 — establish ROI/Return on Expectation. Pragmatic alternative to Kirkpatrick.
**Application:** Stage 1 — define strategic priorities and supporting capability needs. Stage 2 — use mixed methods (KPIs, surveys, stories) to evaluate contribution. Stage 3 — articulate value in financial or strategic terms (ROE often more practical than ROI).
**Case Study:** A UK retailer used Anderson's model for leadership development. Stage 1: identified store manager capability as #1 strategic gap. Stage 2: tracked engagement, NPS, and shrinkage in test/control stores (manager dev participants). Stage 3: Return on Expectation framed in strategic terms — "we built a 200-strong pipeline of ready-now store managers within 18 months, supporting our 40-store expansion plan." This narrative won executive support without complex ROI calculations.
**Expert insight:** Anderson's *Value of Learning* (CIPD, 2007) was developed to make evaluation pragmatic. Particularly valuable when ROI calculation is technically difficult.
**Takeaways:**
- Return on Expectation (ROE) often more useful than pure ROI.
- Strategic narrative complements financial metrics.
- Pragmatism beats methodological purity for stakeholder buy-in.

## 96. Transfer of Learning Strategies
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Strategies to ensure learning is applied to the workplace — the bridge from training to behaviour change. Research consistently shows ~70% of training fails to transfer without deliberate strategies.
**Application:** Transfer strategies: manager involvement before and after; opportunity to apply quickly; coaching follow-up; peer accountability; reminder/booster sessions; goal-setting and tracking; performance management integration; environmental support (tools, time).
**Case Study:** A pharmaceutical firm spent £4M on a sales training initiative. Initial measurement showed flat sales 6 months post-training (no transfer). Root cause analysis: managers didn't reinforce; tools weren't aligned; recognition wasn't tied to new behaviours. Redesign: pre-training manager briefings; post-training coaching plan; revised performance criteria; reps shared "win stories" monthly. Sales rose 11% in next 6 months. Lesson: transfer design > content design.
**Expert insight:** Broad & Newstrom (1992): transfer happens before training (60%), during (20%), after (20%). Will Thalheimer: "Spaced practice" boosts retention 50-200%. Manager support is the #1 transfer factor.
**Takeaways:**
- Design transfer before designing content.
- Manager involvement is non-negotiable.
- Spaced reinforcement multiplies retention.

## 97. Argyris & Schön — Single/Double-Loop Learning
**List:** A
**Definition:** Single-loop learning = correcting errors within existing mental models ("are we doing things right?"). Double-loop learning = questioning the assumptions/values underlying the system ("are we doing the right things?").
**Application:** Most organisations excel at single-loop (continuous improvement, KPI optimisation) but stumble at double-loop (questioning the strategy, examining defensive routines). HR can foster double-loop via after-action reviews with "why" probing, leadership coaching, and psychological safety.
**Expert insight:** Argyris's research at Harvard showed senior leaders consistently fail to engage in double-loop learning due to "defensive routines" (face-saving, blame avoidance). His *Teaching Smart People How to Learn* (HBR 1991) is a classic.
**Takeaways:**
- Most "learning organisations" are stuck in single-loop.
- Defensive routines block double-loop learning.
- Senior leader vulnerability is the unlock.

## 98. Senge's Learning Organisation
**List:** A
**Definition:** Peter Senge's vision: organisations where people continually expand their capacity to create desired results, where new and expansive patterns of thinking are nurtured. Built on five disciplines: Systems Thinking, Personal Mastery, Mental Models, Shared Vision, Team Learning.
**Application:** HR's role: foster personal mastery via development opportunities; surface mental models via dialogue and reflection; build shared vision via co-creation; enable team learning via after-action reviews; teach systems thinking via causal mapping. Senge's "fifth discipline" (systems thinking) is the integrator.
**Expert insight:** Senge's *The Fifth Discipline* (1990) is canonical. Critics argue the concept is more aspirational than operational. SOL (Society for Organisational Learning) is the practitioner community.
**Takeaways:**
- Five disciplines are interdependent, not standalone.
- Systems thinking is the integrative discipline.
- Mental model surfacing is the hardest discipline in practice.

## 99. Knowledge Management — Tacit/Explicit
**List:** A
**Definition:** Nonaka & Takeuchi distinguish explicit knowledge (codifiable, transferable via documents) from tacit knowledge (experiential, hard to articulate). Their SECI model: Socialisation, Externalisation, Combination, Internalisation describes knowledge conversion.
**Application:** Tools: explicit — wikis, knowledge bases, SOPs; tacit — communities of practice, mentoring, apprenticeship, storytelling. SECI design: socialise (apprentice with experts); externalise (capture in case studies); combine (synthesise into models); internalise (apply in practice).
**Expert insight:** Nonaka & Takeuchi's *The Knowledge-Creating Company* (1995) is foundational. Buckman Labs and BP are landmark KM cases. AI now enables semi-automatic tacit knowledge capture via conversational AI.
**Takeaways:**
- Most organisational value is tacit — under-captured.
- Document-only KM systems fail; community-based succeed.
- Loss of tacit knowledge in retirements/restructuring is huge unmeasured cost.

## 100. Career Pathing, Lattices, IDPs
**List:** A
**Definition:** Career Path = sequential roles building expertise/seniority. Career Lattice = multi-directional moves (lateral, diagonal) building breadth. IDP (Individual Development Plan) = personal action plan for capability growth.
**Application:** Career paths suit hierarchical organisations; lattices fit modern, flatter, skills-based organisations. IDPs typically include: career goal; capability gaps; planned actions (training, assignments, mentoring); success metrics; manager support. Annual refresh; quarterly check-ins.
**Expert insight:** Cathy Benko's *The Corporate Lattice* (2010) argues hierarchical careers are obsolete. Gartner's research: lattice-style internal mobility correlates with 60% higher retention. CIPD: 41% of employees who leave cite "no career progression" — IDPs help mitigate.
**Takeaways:**
- Lattices fit modern flat structures better than ladders.
- IDP without manager engagement is paperwork.
- Internal mobility platforms (Gloat, Eightfold) reduce friction.

## 101. Leadership Development Programmes
**List:** A
**Definition:** Structured multi-month programmes building leadership capability — typically blending classroom, 360-feedback, coaching, action learning, simulations, and project work.
**Application:** Design components: capability framework (e.g., Korn Ferry's Leadership Architect); cohort-based learning; senior sponsor involvement; real business projects; sustained coaching; transition support. Common levels: first-time managers; mid-level; senior leaders; executives.
**Expert insight:** McKinsey research: leadership development is the most-invested HR area but only 11% of execs say their programmes work. CCL, INSEAD, Harvard Business School Executive Education are leading providers. Bersin: top-quartile leadership development organisations outperform peers 2x in revenue growth.
**Takeaways:**
- Cohort-based + project-based outperforms classroom-only.
- Senior sponsor engagement is the #1 success factor.
- Pre/post 360-feedback measures behaviour change credibly.

## 102. Mentoring vs Coaching Distinctions
**List:** A
**Definition:** Coaching = goal-focused, time-bound, professional skill (trained coaches). Mentoring = relationship-based, longer-term, knowledge-transfer (senior colleagues). Sponsorship = active advocacy for advancement.
**Application:** Choose by need: coaching for specific performance/development goals; mentoring for career guidance and network building; sponsorship for advancement. Reverse mentoring (junior teaches senior) increasingly used for digital, generational, and DEI learning.
**Expert insight:** ICF: coaching is "thought-partnership" (asking, not telling). David Clutterbuck has codified mentoring methodology. Sylvia Ann Hewlett's research: sponsorship advances careers 23%+ more than mentoring alone.
**Takeaways:**
- Coaching ≠ Mentoring ≠ Sponsorship — clarify in programme design.
- Reverse mentoring works for digital and DEI capability.
- Sponsorship matters more than mentoring for advancement.

## 103. Compliance/Mandatory Training
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Training required by law, regulation, or organisational policy — covering safety, harassment, ethics, data protection, AML, etc. Failure carries legal and reputational consequences.
**Application:** Common compliance training: anti-harassment (mandatory in many US states); GDPR/data protection; anti-bribery (UK Bribery Act); H&S; AML for financial services. Tracking: completion rates, certification renewal cycles, audit trails. Increasingly delivered via LMS with auto-reminders.
**Case Study:** A US bank faced a regulatory enforcement action including a $25M fine partly attributed to insufficient AML training (38% completion among customer-facing staff). Remediation: (1) re-designed AML training (microlearning + scenario-based); (2) integrated completion to compensation eligibility; (3) escalation to manager for non-completion; (4) annual recertification with knowledge testing. Completion rose to 99%; subsequent regulatory inspection cited compliance training as best-in-class.
**Expert insight:** CIPD: 78% of organisations require mandatory compliance training, but only 22% measure actual behaviour change. The temptation: turn it into "check-the-box" — but regulators increasingly probe effectiveness, not just completion.
**Takeaways:**
- Completion ≠ Comprehension ≠ Behaviour Change.
- Regulators now examine effectiveness, not just records.
- Microlearning + scenarios outperform 60-min e-learning modules.

## 104. Digital Badges & Micro-Credentials
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Verifiable, portable digital credentials representing specific skills, achievements, or qualifications — using open standards (Open Badges, Mozilla; Verifiable Credentials, W3C) often blockchain-backed.
**Application:** Use cases: internal skills recognition (replacing "training certificates"); externally portable credentials (IBM SkillsBuild, Google Career Certificates); university micro-degrees; industry certifications (AWS, Salesforce). Increasingly recognised by employers for skills-based hiring.
**Case Study:** IBM launched a digital badge programme in 2015. By 2023: 4 million+ badges issued across 1,800+ skills. Employee uptake: 64% of employees earned at least one badge annually. Business impact: 87% of badge earners reported improved engagement; internal mobility on critical skills (e.g., cloud, AI) accelerated 2x. IBM's open-badge platform now serves multiple Fortune 500 firms.
**Expert insight:** Brandon Hall Group: 87% of L&D leaders predict micro-credentials will be standard within 5 years. WEF Future of Jobs Report: skills-based hiring (often verified via micro-credentials) growing 19% annually.
**Takeaways:**
- Badges only valuable if recognised externally — choose open standards.
- Tie to skills frameworks, not just course completion.
- Skills-based hiring will accelerate badge adoption.

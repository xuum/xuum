# HR Wiki — Domains 12-15: Analytics/Tech, International HR, CSR/Ethics, HR Function Design

## 262. Evidence-Based Practice (EBP) in HR
**List:** A
**Definition:** Making HR decisions using the best available evidence from four sources: scientific research, organisational data, stakeholder input, and professional experience (Briner, Denyer & Rousseau).
**Application:** Process: ASK (precise question) → ACQUIRE (best evidence) → APPRAISE (quality) → AGGREGATE (synthesise) → APPLY (decide) → ASSESS (measure outcome). Distinguishes evidence-based from intuition-based, opinion-based, or fad-based practice.
**Expert insight:** CIPD's Eric Cabello and Rob Briner pioneered EBP in HR. CEBMa (Centre for Evidence-Based Management). Research finds 60% of HR decisions don't use evidence. CIPD's *In Search of the Best Available Evidence*.
**Takeaways:** Four evidence sources; ASK-to-ASSESS cycle; combats fad adoption.

## 263. People Analytics & Predictive Analytics
**List:** A
**Definition:** Application of statistical and ML methods to people data to inform HR decisions. Descriptive (what happened); diagnostic (why); predictive (what will happen); prescriptive (what to do).
**Application:** Use cases: turnover prediction; performance drivers; engagement-business outcome links; pay equity audits; recruitment quality models. Tools: Visier, Workday Prism, Tableau, custom Python/R.
**Expert insight:** David Green (Insight222), Josh Bersin: people analytics maturity correlates with 56% better financial outcomes. McKinsey: only 10% of organisations have advanced people analytics; 50% basic.
**Takeaways:** Maturity model (descriptive→prescriptive); business-question-led; ethical guardrails essential.

## 264. HR Metrics & KPIs
**List:** A
**Definition:** Quantitative measures of HR performance: efficiency (cost/time), effectiveness (quality/outcomes), and strategic (business impact).
**Application:** Core metrics: time-to-fill; cost-per-hire; quality-of-hire; turnover (vol/invol, regrettable); engagement; absence; promotion rate; revenue per employee; HR-to-employee ratio. SHRM HR Metrics, CIPD Workforce Reporting guides.
**Expert insight:** Bersin's "predictive HR metrics" emphasise leading vs lagging. SHRM Benchmarking: median time-to-fill 36 days; cost-per-hire $4,700; turnover 18%.
**Takeaways:** Curate to <20 metrics; mix efficiency + outcome + strategic; benchmark externally.

## 265. Quantitative vs Qualitative Research
**List:** A
**Definition:** Quantitative = numerical data, statistical analysis (surveys, metrics). Qualitative = narrative data, interpretive analysis (interviews, focus groups, observation).
**Application:** Quant for breadth, generalisation, trend analysis. Qual for depth, "why" understanding, theory building. Mixed methods triangulate. Choose by research question, not preference.
**Expert insight:** Creswell's research methods textbook is canonical. CIPD Level 7 emphasises mixed methods. Qual methods often undervalued in data-driven HR cultures.
**Takeaways:** Method follows question; mixed methods often optimal; qual not "soft."

## 266. Survey Design & Administration
**List:** A
**Definition:** Process of designing, fielding, and analysing employee surveys: research design, item construction, sampling, distribution, response management, analysis, action planning.
**Application:** Design: clear objectives; valid items; scales (Likert 5-7 point); pilot testing; length (5-15 min for engagement; 30 for census). Administration: communication; protected anonymity; response targets. Analysis: descriptive + cuts + open-text + benchmarks.
**Expert insight:** Don Dillman's *Tailored Design Method* is standard. Glint, Qualtrics, Peakon dominate platforms. Action on results is the key trust signal — 60% of surveys fail by not closing the loop.
**Takeaways:** Action visibility > question quality; cuts by demographic essential; <15 min max.

## 267. Thematic / Content / Grounded Theory Analysis
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Qualitative analysis methods: Thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke) identifies patterns; Content analysis quantifies word/concept frequency; Grounded theory (Glaser & Strauss) builds theory from data via constant comparison.
**Application:** Used for: exit interview analysis; open-text survey responses; focus group transcripts; engagement comment analysis. Software: NVivo, MAXQDA, Atlas.ti, or AI-powered (ChatGPT/Claude API).
**Case Study:** A UK NHS trust analysed 4,200 open-text engagement survey responses. Manual thematic analysis would have taken 80 person-hours. AI-assisted coding (with human verification): 12 hours; identified 14 themes with frequency. Discovery: 31% of comments referenced shift pattern issues — invisible in quantitative scores. Intervention: shift pattern redesign with employee input. Engagement scores rose 14 points.
**Expert insight:** Braun & Clarke's 6-phase thematic analysis is most widely used. AI-augmented qualitative analysis is rapidly maturing.
**Takeaways:** Open-text data is goldmine; AI accelerates dramatically; verify AI coding.

## 268. Regression & Descriptive Statistics for HR
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Descriptive stats summarise data (mean, median, range, distribution). Regression identifies relationships between variables (e.g., what predicts turnover). Multiple regression handles many predictors simultaneously.
**Application:** HR examples: turnover regression on tenure, manager, location, pay; engagement on manager, autonomy, pay; promotion on performance, demographics (pay equity audit). Tools: Excel, SPSS, R, Python (statsmodels, sklearn).
**Case Study:** A US tech firm regressed turnover (binary) on 28 variables. Significant predictors: manager rating (β=-0.42), pay vs market (β=-0.31), commute distance (β=+0.18), promotion delay (β=+0.22). Intervention focused top 3: manager training, market pay adjustments for hot skills, hybrid work expansion. Turnover dropped 6 points in 12 months.
**Expert insight:** Andy Field's *Discovering Statistics Using R*. McKinsey People Analytics often regression-based.
**Takeaways:** Regression isolates drivers from noise; multicollinearity is risk; statistical ≠ causal.

## 269. Correlation vs Causation in HR
**List:** A
**Definition:** Correlation = variables co-vary statistically. Causation = one variable causes another. Confusing them is the most common HR analytics error.
**Application:** Always ask: third variable? Reverse causation? Selection bias? Methods to establish causation: randomised experiments, natural experiments, longitudinal designs, instrumental variables, difference-in-differences.
**Expert insight:** Pearson's classic warning. Bersin's research: 70% of HR analytics findings cite correlation as causal — leads to misguided interventions. A/B testing increasingly used in HR (e.g., Google's recruiting experiments).
**Takeaways:** Default skepticism on causation claims; A/B test when feasible; longitudinal data > cross-sectional.

## 270. Benchmarking — Internal & External
**List:** A
**Definition:** Comparing HR metrics against internal historical data, peers, or industry standards. Internal benchmarking compares units/teams. External compares to industry/competitors.
**Application:** Sources: SHRM benchmarking; CIPD reports; Mercer, WTW; Glassdoor; LinkedIn Talent Insights. Match comparables: industry, size, geography. Use as directional, not absolute.
**Expert insight:** Beware Goodhart's Law: benchmarks become targets and get gamed. Bersin: benchmarking + diagnosis > benchmarking alone.
**Takeaways:** Use multiple sources; ensure comparability; benchmarks inform — don't dictate.

## 271. Data Visualization & Storytelling

**Expert insight:** Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic's *Storytelling with Data* is canonical. Edward Tufte's principles. McKinsey's pyramid principle for narrative.

## 274. Generative AI & AI Ethics in HR
**List:** A
**Definition:** Generative AI (LLMs like GPT-4, Claude) applied to HR tasks: drafting job descriptions, screening resumes, summarising performance feedback, building learning content, employee chatbots.
**Application:** Use cases: recruiting (drafting JDs, candidate screening — with caution), L&D (course content, micro-content), policy Q&A (chatbots), performance feedback drafting. Risks: bias, hallucination, privacy, IP.

## 275. AI Bias, Transparency, Explainability
**List:** A
**Definition:** AI bias = systematic unfairness in algorithmic outputs (often inheriting societal bias from training data). Transparency = visibility of model logic. Explainability = understanding why a model made a specific decision.
**Application:** Mitigation: diverse training data; bias testing (e.g., disparate impact analysis); explainable models (SHAP values); human review of high-stakes decisions; vendor due diligence; ongoing monitoring.
**Expert insight:** Amazon's failed recruiting AI (2018) penalised women's CVs — case study in inherited bias. Cathy O'Neil's *Weapons of Math Destruction*. ICO/EEOC enforcement intensifying.
**Takeaways:** Bias is technical and ethical; ongoing testing (not one-time); document the human in the loop.

## 276. GDPR & Employee Data Protection
**List:** A
**Definition:** EU/UK General Data Protection Regulation: comprehensive data privacy law. Six lawful bases for processing; data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, portability); 72-hour breach notification; DPIA required for high-risk processing.
**Application:** HR-specific: legal basis for processing usually "legitimate interest" or "contract"; consent rarely appropriate (power imbalance); special category data (health) needs higher justification; record-of-processing required; data subject access requests (SARs) common HR workload.
**Expert insight:** ICO enforcement intensifying. Maximum fines 4% global revenue (£20M+). EU-US Data Privacy Framework (2023) updates transatlantic transfers.
**Takeaways:** Consent not usually right legal basis; SARs are operational burden; vendor DPAs essential.

## 277. CCPA & US State Privacy Laws
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** California Consumer Privacy Act (2018, amended by CPRA 2020): first major US state privacy law. Now joined by 13+ state laws (VA, CO, CT, UT, IA, TX, FL, etc.). Each varies.
**Application:** Employee/applicant rights: notice at collection; access; deletion (limited); right to know; right to correct; opt-out of sale (limited HR context). Patchwork compliance complexity for multi-state employers.
**Case Study:** A multi-state US tech firm faced CCPA enforcement action for failing to provide HR notice at collection and respond to access requests within 45 days. Settlement: $1.2M + comprehensive remediation. Programme deployed: HR-specific privacy notice; SAR response process (parallel to GDPR); state-by-state legal review. Cost: $3.5M ongoing compliance vs $multi-million regulatory exposure.
**Expert insight:** IAPP tracks state laws. American Privacy Rights Act (federal proposal) in negotiation. Fragmentation increasing compliance cost.
**Takeaways:** Multi-state employer faces 13+ frameworks; HR data is in scope; CalOPPA dovetails.

## 278. BYOD, Acceptable Use, Social Media Policies
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** BYOD = Bring Your Own Device policies. Acceptable Use = workplace technology rules. Social Media policies = guidance on workplace-related social media usage.
**Application:** BYOD: device security requirements, MDM enrolment, data separation, reimbursement. Acceptable Use: prohibited activities, monitoring disclosure, consequences. Social Media: must not impair NLRA Section 7 rights (concerted activity); reasonable restrictions on confidentiality and defamation.
**Case Study:** A US retailer's social media policy prohibited "discussing terms and conditions of employment online." NLRB charged Section 7 violation — overly broad. Settlement: policy revised; affected employees made whole. New policy narrowly tailored: prohibit defamation, confidential info disclosure; explicitly permits employee discussion of pay/conditions. Lesson: post-Stericycle (2023), policies must be narrowly drafted.
**Expert insight:** NLRB Stericycle (2023) reinstated stricter "reasonable employee" test for handbook policies. SHRM: 64% of US firms have BYOD policies; 90% have social media policies.
**Takeaways:** Narrow drafting essential post-Stericycle; balance security with Section 7.

## 279. Workplace Monitoring / Surveillance Ethics
**List:** A
**Definition:** Use of technology to monitor employee activity: keystroke logging, screen capture, productivity scoring, video, audio, biometric. Legal in most jurisdictions with notice; ethically contested.
**Application:** Legitimate uses: safety, compliance, security, productivity insight. Disclosed monitoring less ethically problematic. EU/UK requires proportionality; US generally permits with notice. ECPA exceptions allow business-purpose monitoring.
**Expert insight:** UK ICO surveillance code: proportionality essential. CIPD Wellbeing research: monitoring without consent corrodes trust. McKinsey: 60% of remote workers feel monitored more post-COVID; engagement drops 25% with intrusive monitoring.
**Takeaways:** Transparent > covert; proportionate to purpose; trust costs > productivity gains often.

## 281. Research Philosophy — Positivism, Interpretivism
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Positivism = research philosophy assuming objective reality, measurable via quantitative methods (natural science model). Interpretivism = subjective reality constructed by humans, accessed via qualitative methods.
**Application:** Choice affects research design: positivism → surveys, experiments, statistical inference; interpretivism → interviews, ethnography, narrative analysis. Pragmatism = use methods fit for question (most practical HR research).
**Case Study:** A CIPD Level 7 candidate researching "why women leave engineering at year 5" tried positivist approach first: large-scale survey on factors. Findings vague — items pre-determined by researcher's assumptions. Switched to interpretivist semi-structured interviews; surfaced unexpected themes (microaggressions, exclusion from after-work networks). Mixed-methods approach combined survey breadth with interview depth — most insightful design.
**Expert insight:** Saunders, Lewis, Thornhill's research methods textbook is CIPD standard. Pragmatism dominant in HR research.
**Takeaways:** Philosophy shapes method; pragmatism common; mixed methods often best.

## 282. Sampling Strategies
**List:** A
**Definition:** Methods for selecting research participants: Probability (random, stratified, cluster) for generalisability; Non-probability (convenience, purposive, snowball) for accessibility/specificity.
**Application:** HR contexts: census surveys for small populations; stratified sampling for cross-segments (department, level, demographics); purposive sampling for case studies. Sample size: depends on population, variance, desired confidence.
**Expert insight:** Cochran's sample size formula; G*Power for power analysis. CIPD Level 7 requires methodological justification.
**Takeaways:** Match strategy to purpose; sample size affects power; non-probability has limits.

## 283. Action Research Methodology
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Kurt Lewin's research approach: cycles of plan-act-observe-reflect, conducted with (not on) the system. Combines knowledge production with change implementation.
**Application:** Especially suited for OD interventions: diagnose with stakeholders → plan intervention → implement → study impact → reflect → adjust → repeat. Participants are co-researchers, not subjects.
**Case Study:** A UK NHS trust's OD team conducted action research on a new ward team model. Cycle 1: diagnose current dynamics with team; Cycle 2: introduce new structure; Cycle 3: observe outcomes; Cycle 4: refine. Six cycles over 12 months. Outcomes: better than top-down rollout in three peer wards. Team felt ownership; researchers learned what didn't fit theory.
**Expert insight:** Reason & Bradbury's *Handbook of Action Research*. Coghlan's *Doing Action Research in Your Own Organization*.
**Takeaways:** Iterative, participatory, dual-purpose; fits OD work; slower but stickier.

## 284. Triangulation Methods
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Using multiple methods, sources, investigators, or theories to study a phenomenon — increases validity, reveals different facets, builds robustness.
**Application:** Types (Denzin): data triangulation (multiple sources); method triangulation (quant + qual); investigator triangulation (multiple researchers); theory triangulation (multiple frameworks). In HR: survey + interviews + analytics + observation.
**Case Study:** An engagement diagnosis used: (1) census survey (quant); (2) focus groups (qual); (3) exit interview analysis; (4) absence/turnover analytics; (5) leadership interviews. Survey showed pay as top issue (40% mention); focus groups revealed underlying issue was perceived unfairness in promotion, not absolute pay levels. Different sources surfaced different layers; intervention focused on promotion transparency, not pay raises.
**Expert insight:** Multiple-method designs are CIPD Level 7 best practice. Some quantitative purists critique as methodologically mixed.
**Takeaways:** Different methods reveal different truths; convergent findings strongest; design intentionally.

## 285. Business Research Project Methodology
**List:** A
**Definition:** Structured approach to applied research: problem identification → literature review → research questions → methodology → data collection → analysis → findings → recommendations → implementation plan.
**Application:** CIPD Level 7 7CO04 unit. Quality criteria: critical literature review; methodology fit-to-purpose; rigorous analysis; evidence-based recommendations; pragmatic action plan; impact measurement design.
**Expert insight:** Saunders et al.'s research onion model is standard. CIPD provides extensive guidance via 7CO04 module.
**Takeaways:** Methodology rigour matters; recommendations are deliverable, not just findings; impact-focused.

## 286. Global HR Strategy — Standardisation vs Localisation
**List:** A
**Definition:** Strategic choice for MNCs: standardise HR practices globally (efficiency, brand consistency, mobility) vs localise (cultural fit, legal compliance, local market). Most use "glocal" hybrid.
**Application:** Standardise: core values, leadership competencies, ethics, basic talent processes. Localise: pay, benefits, hiring practices, statutory requirements, day-to-day management. Framework: "non-negotiables vs adaptable."
**Expert insight:** Bartlett & Ghoshal's transnational solution (1989) frames the strategic choice. Brewster's IHRM research. CIPD: 78% of MNCs report standardisation-localisation tension.
**Takeaways:** Glocal hybrid is norm; clarify non-negotiables explicitly; respect local expertise.

## 287. IHRM Approaches — Ethnocentric, Polycentric, Geocentric, Regiocentric
**List:** A
**Definition:** Perlmutter's typology of MNC staffing/HR: Ethnocentric (HQ nationals dominate); Polycentric (host-country nationals lead local ops); Geocentric (best person regardless of nationality); Regiocentric (regional staffing).
**Application:** Choice affects recruitment, mobility, compensation, leadership development. Ethnocentric easier early; polycentric fits local responsiveness; geocentric meritocratic but complex; regiocentric balances.
**Expert insight:** Perlmutter (1969) classic framework. Most MNCs evolve from ethnocentric to geocentric over time. Bartlett & Ghoshal's "transnational" extends Perlmutter.
**Takeaways:** No single best; matches strategy and maturity; geocentric requires global mobility.

## 288. International Organisational Structures
**List:** A
**Definition:** MNC structures: Global (centralised, efficiency); Multinational (decentralised, local responsiveness); International (knowledge transfer from HQ); Transnational (combined efficiency + responsiveness + learning — Bartlett & Ghoshal).
**Application:** Structure choice driven by industry pressures: cost (global), local responsiveness (multinational), both (transnational). HR design follows structure: roles, careers, mobility, decision rights.
**Expert insight:** Bartlett & Ghoshal's *Managing Across Borders* (1989) is foundational. Few firms achieve true transnational form.
**Takeaways:** Structure follows strategic imperative; transnational is aspirational; HR design implications major.

## 289. Varieties of Capitalism — CME/LME
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Hall & Soskice's classification: Coordinated Market Economies (Germany, Japan, Nordics — long-termist, stakeholder, coordinated training) vs Liberal Market Economies (US, UK — shareholder primacy, market-based labour, mobility-based skills).
**Application:** Affects HR practice: CME — long-tenured employment, vocational training, works councils; LME — at-will, market-rate skills, shareholder pressure on costs. MNCs face strategic choice when operating across.
**Case Study:** A UK firm acquiring a German manufacturer in 2019 expected to apply LME-style cost reductions. German Betriebsrat (works council) and Mitbestimmung (co-determination) law required consultation. Initial restructuring proposal blocked; modified plan emerged through 9 months of negotiation. Outcome better than the original plan (retained key talent, smoother integration). Lesson: CME institutional context cannot be bulldozed by LME assumptions.
**Expert insight:** Hall & Soskice's *Varieties of Capitalism* (2001). Tony Edwards's research on MNC HR cross-border.
**Takeaways:** Institutional context matters; CME requires longer planning horizons; LME assumptions don't transfer.

## 290. Expatriate Management & Cross-Cultural Training
**List:** A
**Definition:** End-to-end management of international assignments: selection, preparation, in-country support, repatriation. Cross-cultural training equips expatriates and families for cultural transition.
**Application:** Selection: technical skill + cultural agility + family situation. Preparation: language, cultural training, logistics. In-country: mentor, ongoing support, performance management. Repatriation: re-entry planning, role placement, retention.
**Expert insight:** Mercer Global Mobility surveys. Tung's expatriate research (selection criteria, failure rates). Failure rate 15-25% — typically partner/family adjustment.
**Takeaways:** Family adjustment is biggest failure factor; repatriation often worse than departure; structured programmes pay off.

## 291. Expatriate Compensation — Balance Sheet Approach
**List:** A
**Definition:** Most common expatriate compensation method: maintain expat's home-country purchasing power by paying home-country base + COL, housing, hardship, mobility premiums; tax equalisation.
**Application:** Components: base (home-country); goods & services (COLA differential); housing (host); income tax (equalised); contingency premiums. Alternatives: localisation (host-country pay), local-plus, lump sum, ALDI.
**Expert insight:** Mercer, ECA International publish COL data globally. Balance sheet maintains "no win, no loss" — expat doesn't profit or suffer from assignment. Major cost ($300K-$1M+ per expat per year all-in).
**Takeaways:** Balance sheet is standard but expensive; alternatives growing (localisation, local-plus); transparency important.

## 292. Tax Equalisation & Tax Protection
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Tax Equalisation = expat pays "hypothetical tax" (as if at home); employer covers actual host taxes (savings to employer if host lower, cost if higher). Tax Protection = expat pays actual host tax; employer reimburses excess vs hypothetical.
**Application:** Tax equalisation most common (consistent expat treatment); tax protection complex but cheaper for assignments to lower-tax countries. Specialist providers (KPMG, EY, BDO) manage. Requires hypothetical tax calculation, true-up annual.
**Case Study:** A US firm assigned a US executive to UK on $400K package. UK income tax + NI ~45%; US federal + state ~30%. Under equalisation: employer paid $60K additional tax cost annually. Switched to tax protection for future UK assignments: $0 additional cost when host taxes higher; employee bore tax burden. Trade-off: more attractive to expat under equalisation; cheaper to employer under protection. Hybrid policies emerged.
**Expert insight:** Mercer Global Tax Survey. International Tax & Compensation Specialists (KPMG, EY, BDO) handle complexity.
**Takeaways:** Choose method strategically; equalisation more expat-friendly; protection cheaper to employer.

## 293. Repatriation Planning
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Structured process for returning expatriates to home country: role placement, career continuity, cultural re-adjustment, family support. Often the most neglected phase, with highest retention impact.
**Application:** Components: pre-return planning (12-month horizon); role identification; reverse culture shock support; career conversation; pay & benefits reconciliation; family resettlement. Mentor system continuity.
**Case Study:** A multinational lost 35% of repatriates within 18 months of return — taking knowledge and relationships with them. Audit: no structured repatriation; expatriates "fell into" available roles; pay drops; family struggled with reverse culture shock. Programme launched: 12-month repatriation planning; role match committee; financial transition support; family resettlement counselling; alumni network for expats. Retention rose to 78% at 18 months.
**Expert insight:** Brookfield Global Relocation Trends consistently identifies repatriation as #1 expat dissatisfaction area. KPMG reports 25-50% repatriate turnover within 2 years industry-wide.
**Takeaways:** Repatriation is biggest mobility leak; structured planning essential; family + role + pay all matter.

## 294. Third-Country Nationals (TCNs)
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Employees who are neither home-country nor host-country nationals — e.g., a German citizen working in Mexico for a US firm. Create compensation, tax, immigration complexity.
**Application:** TCN compensation often follows host-country (localised) or home-country (balance sheet). Tax: complex (potentially three jurisdictions). Immigration: visa from host; sometimes work permit constraints.
**Case Study:** A global energy firm had 280 TCNs across 60 countries. Inconsistent compensation policies: some on home-country balance sheet, others localised, others ad-hoc. Disparity created internal equity issues; some TCNs earned 3x peers in same role. Programme rationalised: clear TCN policy (typically home-country balance sheet for senior; localised for mid-level); transparent communication; transition period for adjustments. Result: equity restored; small cost increase offset by attrition reduction.
**Expert insight:** Mercer Global Mobility: TCN segment is fastest-growing in international assignments. Policy clarity critical.
**Takeaways:** TCN policies often inconsistent — rationalise; tax complexity high; transparent rules essential.

## 295. Cross-Cultural Communication & CQ
**List:** A
**Definition:** Cultural Intelligence (CQ — Earley & Ang): capability to function effectively across cultural contexts. Four dimensions: CQ Drive (motivation), CQ Knowledge (cultural understanding), CQ Strategy (planning), CQ Action (behavioural flexibility).
**Application:** Develop via: exposure (international assignments, diverse teams); structured learning (cross-cultural training); coaching; reflection. Increasingly required for global leadership roles. CQ assessment available (Cultural Intelligence Center).
**Expert insight:** CQ has stronger predictive validity than IQ for global leadership effectiveness. INSEAD's Erin Meyer's *The Culture Map* (2014) widely used in practice. SHRM-CP Inclusive Mindset includes Cultural Agility.
**Takeaways:** CQ trainable; CQ Action is hardest; assessment + development plan effective.

## 296. ILO Core Labour Standards
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** International Labour Organization's eight Core Conventions covering four fundamental principles: freedom of association/collective bargaining; elimination of forced labour; abolition of child labour; elimination of discrimination in employment.
**Application:** MNCs operating across jurisdictions must respect ILO standards regardless of local law minima. Supply chain audits address core standards. Modern slavery laws (UK 2015, Australia 2018, EU CSDDD 2024) make ILO compliance commercial imperative.
**Case Study:** A UK retailer's annual Modern Slavery Statement (required by Modern Slavery Act 2015) revealed forced labour risk in two tier-3 suppliers in South Asia. Audit triggered: ILO Convention 29 (Forced Labour) violations identified; suppliers given remediation plans (worker consent verification, fee reimbursement, free movement); two non-compliant suppliers dropped. Cost: £3M plus reputation impact avoided.
**Expert insight:** ILO standards underpin UN Global Compact, OECD Guidelines, EU CSDDD. Supply chain accountability is intensifying.
**Takeaways:** ILO standards transcend local law; modern slavery legislation creates real liability; supply chain due diligence is HR-relevant.

## 297. International Employment Law Variations
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** Substantive differences in employment law across jurisdictions — covering termination, working time, leave, collective rights, employee benefits, data protection.
**Application:** Key variations: notice periods (UK statutory 1-12 weeks; Germany 4 weeks to 7 months by tenure; US generally at-will); severance pay (mandatory in EU, optional in US); maternity leave (UK 52 weeks, US FMLA 12 weeks unpaid). MNCs need country-specific HR knowledge or specialist providers.
**Case Study:** A US firm acquired French subsidiary, attempted to dismiss 8 employees citing performance. France employment law requires "just cause" + works council consultation + extended notice. Improper procedure: tribunal awarded reinstatement + €240K in damages. Lesson: country-specific legal counsel essential pre-restructuring.
**Expert insight:** L&E Global, EY, KPMG country guides. IUS Laboris and Ius Laboris networks. Country-specific advisors essential.
**Takeaways:** Country variation is vast; default to local counsel; misjudge at multi-million cost.

## 298. European Works Councils
**List:** B (Case Study)
**Definition:** EU Directive 94/45/EC (recast 2009/38/EC): MNCs with 1,000+ employees and 150+ in at least two EU member states must establish European Works Council on employee request.
**Application:** EWC duties: information and consultation on transnational matters (mergers, restructuring, major investments). Annual plenary meeting; select committee; specialised committees as needed. Funding by company.
**Case Study:** A US-headquartered MNC announced European restructuring (3 plant closures, 1,200 redundancies) without informing EWC. EWC complained to European Commission; injunction blocked restructuring; €2.8M fine; restructuring delayed 8 months pending consultation. CHRO learning: transnational decisions trigger EWC consultation duty even if announced by US HQ.
**Expert insight:** EWC reform debated 2024-25 for stronger consultation rights. ETUC (European Trade Union Confederation) advocates strengthening.
**Takeaways:** Transnational scope triggers EWC consultation; HQ decisions affecting EU need dialogue; reform tightening.

## 299. Global Mobility Management
